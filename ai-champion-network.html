Proposed work sample · Fictional pilot

Launching an AI champion network at Solink

In my first 90 days, I’d ask two departmental champions to work through one decision together. Can a support team use AI to update internal guidance faster while keeping every instruction traceable to an approved source?

This fictional scenario uses invented support material. The workflow, staffing, tools and targets are proposals I’d discuss with Solink.

Days 1–30 · Choose the pilot and set a baseline

I’d agree on a sponsor in People & Culture and ask Support and a second department to nominate a champion each, with time agreed by their managers. We’d meet weekly for 30 minutes. Each champion would bring a question from colleagues or a draft that needed a decision. I’d record the decision, owner and next review date in a shared pilot log.

For the first pilot, a Support writer would turn one approved troubleshooting note into a draft internal knowledge-base update. The support lead would own the workflow and publication decision. Technology and Security would clear the tool and permitted data with the data owner, consulting Legal where needed. Only approved notes would enter the tool. Customer footage, account details and raw tickets would stay outside the exercise.

I’d own evaluation. Before training, I’d ask writers and reviewers to time comparable manual updates, logging active minutes separately for writing and review. We’d record note length and complexity, agree what counts as a critical defect, and use the same approval checklist for both methods.

Days 31–60 · Teach one support workflow

The Support champion would demonstrate how to draft from an approved note. Writers would practise tracing each sentence back to its source. The support lead would practise returning an unsupported draft and would have to record approval before anyone could publish it. I’d hold weekly office hours and use questions from participants to revise the guide.

Champion’s facilitator guide · 15 minutes

0–3 minutes. Give each pair the fictional note below. Assign one writer and one reviewer. Ask the writer to underline every action the note authorizes.

Approved practice note
If a recording is missing from the timeline, check the selected camera and time range. If the gap remains, record the affected interval and escalate to Support.

3–8 minutes. Show the draft. Ask the reviewer to point to the source for each sentence and circle anything they cannot support.

Practice draft
Check the selected camera and time range. If the gap remains, record the affected interval and escalate to Support. Missing recordings will automatically return.

8–12 minutes. Have the pair decide whether to approve, then edit. The reviewer should return this draft. Its final sentence promises recovery, while the note only authorizes checks and escalation. Delete the final sentence and retain the first two. Have the reviewer check the revision against the note.

12–15 minutes. Ask the reviewer to explain the decision. Pass if the pair catches and removes the recovery promise, retains both checks and the escalation instructions, and holds publication for support-lead approval. Fail if any condition is missed. Return the draft for another attempt.

Days 61–90 · Decide whether to expand

I’d review three measures with the champions each week and take the evidence to the sponsor at day 90.

  • Repeat use. From training attendance, eligible assignments and draft logs, count trained writers who used the workflow this week and had used it in a prior week. Divide by trained writers with an eligible assignment this week. Report the counts alongside the percentage.
  • Draft-plus-review time. For each update, add the writer’s and reviewer’s active minutes, including corrections through approval. Compare the median with manual updates of similar length and complexity.
  • Critical defects. Reviewers would log unsupported recovery promises, unsafe instructions or restricted data in a draft. A second reviewer would audit approved updates against their source and log defects that passed approval. I’d report counts separately by stage alongside the number reviewed.

Proposed decision criteria. I’d recommend a limited expansion after four consecutive weeks with at least 60% repeat use, a median time at least 15% below the matched manual baseline, and zero critical defects after approval. If work volume is too low for a meaningful comparison, I’d extend the trial. If repeat use or time misses its target, or reviewers repeatedly catch the same error, I’d revise the exercise with the champion and retest. Any critical defect found after approval or any restricted data entered into the tool would pause the pilot for review.

For a second trial, a People & Culture champion could adapt the exercise to drafting an onboarding FAQ from an approved policy. They’d select their own source and approver and plant an unsupported eligibility promise in the practice draft. I’d share the facilitator guide and measurement sheet, then ask that champion to run the session and collect a fresh manual baseline for their department.
