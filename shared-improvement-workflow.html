Real case study

How I built a shared improvement workflow at SmartONE

30 issues and ideas submitted · 18 fully resolved · 12 HVAC systems and configurations documented.

We launched the workflow around March 2025. It was still in use in November 2026, when I took the ticket counts above.

Giving investigations a shared starting point

Our operations teams investigated issues and developed fixes independently. Findings lived in Microsoft Loop, email and Teams. A colleague working on another building could run into the same problem without knowing who had already investigated it.

I spoke with the people doing the work and secured approval from the COO and Director of Engineering. I partnered with Product to add an Operations Process Improvement form to our existing employee portal. Each submission created a Jira ticket with the issue category, building or project, and project stage. That gave us enough context to discuss it together.

Making time for decisions every week

In the first few weekly engineering meetings, I demonstrated the workflow and trained the team to use it. Then I reserved the last 20–30 minutes of each meeting for ticket review. The form gave people a place to raise an issue. The recurring review gave us a time to decide what to do about it.

We agreed on priorities together. I assigned work to colleagues whose experience fit the issue, recorded the next action, and tagged both the person responsible and the original requester. At the next review we could return to a named owner and a specific action.

The submissions showed what different teams needed. Customer Support raised a handover gap affecting technicians on service calls. Field Service flagged inconsistent records when hardware moved between projects or went out as an emergency replacement. A project manager raised a gap in HVAC integration documentation.

A reference for 12 HVAC systems and configurations

One initiative we completed was a shared installation and troubleshooting reference. The team consolidated guidance on cabling, connection types, control-board settings, temperature setpoints and common faults for 12 HVAC systems and configurations.

We designed it so a mechanical contractor could look up a connection or setting during installation, a new team member could learn how a configuration worked, and Customer Support could consult the same guidance when investigating a fault.
